Crassulacean acid metabolism · Opuntia ficus-indica

Opuntia CAM Clock: night intake, day spending

Prickly pear keeps its stomata shut through the desert day. It takes in CO₂ at night, when cool air costs little water, and parks it as malic acid in large vacuoles. After sunrise it releases that CO₂ behind closed pores and runs the Calvin cycle. Drag the clock to move through the four CAM phases.

Biochemical flow

The same cell runs two programs separated in time. The active one brightens as you move the clock.

Night · stomata open

Active
  1. StomaCO₂ diffuses in

    Cool night air means a small humidity gap, so the open pore loses little water.

  2. CytosolCarbonic anhydrase

    CO₂ is hydrated to bicarbonate (HCO₃⁻).

  3. CytosolPEP carboxylase fixation

    HCO₃⁻ is added to phosphoenolpyruvate. PEP comes from yesterday's starch.

  4. CytosolOxaloacetate (OAA)

    A four-carbon acid, the first product of fixation.

  5. CytosolMalate dehydrogenase → malic acid

    OAA is reduced to malate using NADH.

  6. VacuoleStored in the central vacuole

    Proton pumps load malic acid across the tonoplast. Vacuolar pH falls toward 3.5.

Day · stomata closed

Idle
  1. Guard cellsStomata seal shut

    Hot, dry air would pull water out of an open pore, so the pad closes.

  2. TonoplastMalic acid leaves the vacuole

    Stored acid is released back into the cytosol through the morning.

  3. CytosolDecarboxylation

    Malic enzyme splits malate into CO₂ and pyruvate.

  4. Leaf airCO₂ builds up inside

    With pores closed, internal CO₂ rises well above outside air, which suppresses photorespiration.

  5. ChloroplastRubisco · C3 Calvin cycle

    Light reactions supply ATP and NADPH to fix the released CO₂.

  6. ChloroplastStarch production

    Sugars are stored as starch, the PEP supply for the coming night.

Curves follow the four textbook CAM phases: I night uptake, II dawn transition, III midday closure with decarboxylation, IV late-afternoon reopening with direct Rubisco fixation. Values are an illustrative model, not measurements; real malate peaks and timing vary with water status, temperature and species. Some CAM plants decarboxylate with PEP carboxykinase instead of malic enzyme.
